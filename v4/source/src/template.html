Schrödinger’s Bullet Hell

A. Cat,1,2 B. Box,2 and the Copenhagen Interpretation3 1The Box   2Also the Box   3Disputed

AbstractWe present a twin-stick shooter in which the player is a cat in coherent superposition of up to sixteen positions (256, briefly, with help). Every bullet that touches the cat is a projective measurement, and survival follows the Born rule. Ten experiments suspend twenty-five laws of physics and end in ten bosses. We find no evidence that this is a good idea.

Field guide

The rules of the experiment. Everything you discover goes in the Lab notes.

You are Schrödinger’s cat, and you can be in several places at once.

  1. Split to become 2 cats, then 4, 8 and 16. Every cat fires, so more cats means a lot more firepower. New cats can’t be hit for a moment while they fly out (they look faded).
  2. When a bullet or enemy touches any of your cats, the universe measures you. With N cats you die with probability 1/N. If you survive, only the cat that was touched disappears. With a single cat, a hit is certain death.
  3. Staying split drains coherence (the bar under your cats’ histogram). Enemies near you drain it faster. When it hits zero, you collapse into one cat at random. That is decoherence.
  4. Fold before that happens: every cat merges back into one and releases a shockwave that wipes out nearby bullets and hurts enemies. The more cats you fold, the bigger the blast, and you get some coherence back.
  5. A fold is also a dodge. You collapse onto the cat furthest in the direction you are steering, and you can’t be hit while it happens. Fold just as something is about to hit you for a perfect fold: a bigger blast and coherence back.
  6. Sprint: hold Alt (or » on touch) to run faster, the opposite of focus. It starts with a small burst, and drains coherence for as long as you hold it: moving fast couples you to the environment, more the more cats you are. Tap it instead for a bigger burst, a dash. Neither gives any protection.
  7. Hold focus to move slowly, pull your cats into a tight formation and show their hitboxes. Use it to thread gaps.
  8. Enemies aim at your expectation value ⟨x⟩, the average position of all your cats. When you are spread out, that average can be empty space.
  9. Every wave suspends a law of physics. The card at the start of the wave names it, and the line under the wave name at the top of the screen says what it does. Every fourth wave is a boss.
  10. Everything you meet is written up in your Lab notes: first sighting, observations, a hypothesis, and a conclusion once you have beaten it, with the real physics behind it.

p. 4

Modes

Campaign. Ten experiments of four waves: three with laws suspended, then a boss. Beat the tenth boss, The Apparatus, to open the box. You can start from any experiment you have reached.

Research programme. A new map every run. Choose your way through branching rooms (experiments, risky experiments, anomalies, a grant office, peer review, conferences) to a boss at the end of each of three grant cycles. Funding is spent inside the run and is gone when it ends. Win and the run is published; carry on into Tenure track if you dare.

Endless. Random combinations of laws, elites, space weather and a random boss every fifth wave. It never stops getting harder.

Boss rush. Every boss back to back, each with a random law suspended, then around again, tougher.

Difficulty

Six levels: High School (for learning), Undergrad, Graduate, Postdoc, Reviewer 2 and Nobel Committee. Harder settings shorten every warning, narrow the gaps in bullet walls, and multiply your score and citations. Elites and space weather only appear from Graduate upward, and every boss learns an extra attack on Postdoc and another on Reviewer 2. Endless, Boss rush and the Research programme open when you finish the campaign on Undergrad or harder; Nobel Committee opens when you finish it on Reviewer 2.

Interpretations

After your first boss you choose how to read quantum mechanics. It lasts the whole run and decides which discoveries you are offered.

Lab notes

Every enemy, elite, boss, law, event and black hole gets a page. See something once, in any run, and it is written down for good: a first sighting, then one observation per behaviour or attack, then a hypothesis once you have seen enough, and a conclusion (beat the boss, or defeat enough of an enemy). Every conclusion comes with a footnote on the real physics. That is volume I, Observations. Volume II, Records, keeps your results for every mode and difficulty, tallies (what you destroyed, what got you, which laws you outlasted), lifetime totals, the lineage of named cats that survived, a logbook of recent runs, and your papers. Runs played with master access on are not recorded.

Medals and the cabinet

Medals come only from mastery: beating a boss without being measured, finishing the campaign on a harder difficulty, beating your best score by a clear margin. Spend them in the cabinet on cat designs, bullet trails and colour palettes.

Discoveries (during a run)

Every boss you beat publishes a paper, and you choose one of three discoveries that last for the rest of the run. The pause menu lists the ones you have.

Citations and grants (permanent)

Every run earns citations based on score, waves cleared and bosses beaten, scaled by difficulty. The first time you beat each boss pays extra, and so does every publication (achievement). Spend citations in the grant office on permanent upgrades. You can switch grants off for a pure run, or refund them at any time.

Speedrun splits

Switch on speedrun splits in Settings for a timer per wave, compared with your personal best (green is ahead) and your best ever time for that wave (gold).

Saving

Records, notes, statistics, citations and settings are saved in this browser automatically. To move them to another device or browser, use Export save in Settings and paste the code into Import save there.
