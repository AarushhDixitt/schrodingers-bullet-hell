AbstractWe present a twin-stick shooter in which the player is a cat in coherent superposition of up to sixteen positions. Every bullet that touches the cat is a projective measurement, and survival follows the Born rule. Each wave suspends one law of physics. We find no evidence that this is a good idea.

Field guide

Supplementary material. Everything on the screen, explained.

You are Schrödinger’s cat, and you can be in several places at once.

  1. Split to become 2 cats, then 4, 8 and 16. Every cat fires, so more cats means a lot more firepower.
  2. When a bullet or enemy touches any of your cats, the universe measures you. With N cats you die with probability 1/N. If you survive, only the cat that was touched disappears. With a single cat, a hit is certain death.
  3. Staying split drains coherence (the bar under your cats’ histogram). Enemies near you drain it faster. When it hits zero, you collapse into one cat at random. That is decoherence.
  4. Fold before that happens: every cat merges back into one and releases a shockwave that wipes out nearby bullets and hurts enemies. The more cats you fold, the bigger the blast, and you get some coherence back.
  5. Enemies aim at your expectation value ⟨x⟩, the average position of all your cats. When you are spread out, that average can be empty space.
  6. Every wave suspends one law of physics. The card at the start of the wave names it, and the line under the wave name at the top of the screen says what it does.

  • One bar per possible cat. The colour is that cat’s quantum phase. An empty slot is a cat you lost to a measurement.
  • P(dead | hit) is the chance that the next hit kills you. Lower is safer.
  • Coherence drains while you are split. T₂ is how many seconds you have left at the current drain. Fold before it runs out.

  • Time slows down while the universe decides. The needle lands on MISSED (you survive and lose only the touched cat) or HIT (you die).
